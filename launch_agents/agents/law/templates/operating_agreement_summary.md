## Management and voting

The Company is managed by its members. Day-to-day decisions can be made by any member; major decisions (taking on debt, selling the business, admitting a new member, changing this agreement) need the approval of members holding a majority of the units. Votes are counted in proportion to units held.

## Profits, losses and taxes

Profits and losses are shared in proportion to units held. By default the IRS treats an LLC as a pass-through (a partnership when it has several members): the Company itself pays no federal income tax and each member reports their share on their own return. The members may elect corporate tax treatment later.

## Distributions

The members decide when to distribute cash. Distributions are made in proportion to units held, after the Company keeps enough to pay its debts.

## Transfers and leaving

A member who wants to sell or give away units must first offer them to the Company and the other members (a right of first refusal). A new member joins only with the approval described above.

## Liability

Members are generally not personally responsible for the Company's debts, as long as the Company's money is kept separate from their own.
